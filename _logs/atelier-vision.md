> "An atelier is the private workshop or studio of a professional artist in the fine or decorative arts or an architect, where a principal master and a number of assistants, students, and apprentices can work together producing fine art or visual art released under the master's name or supervision." [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelier)

The thing that started this is simple and a little annoying. Every tool out there wants to be a bridge now. Not a tool. A bridge. You don't talk to the thing anymore. You talk to a wrapper around a description of a bridge to the thing. And the wrapper has it's own wrapper sometimes. And there's servers for the bridges and protocols for the servers and a whole registry of little handshakes to know before you get anything done.

It should not be this hard.

This should be obvious. You hand an agent something that works. A real program. A real binary that does a real thing. You give it a place to run, a sandbox, a little room of its own, and you get out of the way. One program, one box, go. That's been the whole job the whole time. The model looks at the thing, pokes it, reads what comes back, tries again. Like a person would. Like a person does.

Somewhere along the way we forgot. We added three more layers and called it progress, and we patted ourselves on the back.

Every abstraction is a tax. Another thing to keep in your head. Another place it breaks at the worst moment. Another doc to rewire every time the tool updates, which is always. The agent pays the tax too, worse than we do. A system prompt stuffed with skill instructions, step by step instructions, examples of examples, that's just you doing the thinking the model should do. We are over helping. We're smothering it. And it shows. In the output. In the fragility. It works great in the demo and dies the minute the real world shows up. Every time.

The closer we get to handing the agent the real thing and stepping back, the closer our runs get to real doing. Real understanding. You can't shortcut your way to general ability with more specific instructions. Generality doesn't come from a bigger manual. It comes from fewer crutches. Not more. A thing that can do everything with nothing but the tool in front of it. That's what we're after. That's the only road that runs toward anything like AGI, and we're on it or we're not. I like a world with it. So I'll make a contribution. Even a small one.

So what do we want. It's small. A tool. A sandbox. A person watching. That's the whole inventory. The human sees the thing on the screen, live, real, no screenshot of a screenshot. The human points at it and says the word, "no, the seat, the legs are too long," and the agent takes that and turns it into the thing. No ceremony. No plumbing museum between you and the output. Just you, the agent, and the program doing the work in the middle. That's it. That's the whole vision.

Build an atelier for agents.
